Overview

Transforming Rescued Fruits into Nutritious Possibilities

Project Amulya is a sustainability-driven initiative that transforms rescued fruits — sourced from local vendors and markets where they would otherwise be discarded — into high-quality, healthy snack products.

By offering fair monetary compensation, the project creates an additional income stream for vendors while reducing food waste. Production is carried out in collaboration with NGO-run kitchens, generating meaningful livelihood opportunities for women and underserved communities.

The final products are positioned as natural, preservative-free, and health-conscious, while remaining affordable compared to conventional snack brands. Through this integrated model, Project Amulya delivers a compelling blend of environmental responsibility, social impact, and accessible nutrition for all.

The Challenge

Problems Addressed

A large amount of edible produce is wasted daily due to excess stock, appearance defects, and low demand — leading to vendor losses and environmental pollution. Project Amulya intercepts this waste stream and converts it into value.

How It Works

Execution Model

From surplus to shelf in five structured steps

1

Identify Surplus Produce

  • Identifying and collecting surplus produce from farms and wholesale markets
2

Segregation

  • Sorting fruits into edible, partially edible, and non-edible categories
  • Selecting seasonal fruits with a zero-waste proposition
3

Community Processing

  • Engaging local community groups for safe, value-based conversion
4

Packing & Distribution

  • Simple packaging, sold back into the local market at affordable prices
5

Track & Refine

  • Hyper feedback loops refining making and selling strategies
Where We Are

Current Status

Project Amulya has successfully completed its ideation, research, and planning phase. The product portfolio, sourcing strategy, operational framework, branding concepts, and impact model have been comprehensively developed and documented.

The project is currently focused on establishing the partnerships and supply-chain linkages required for implementation — including collaborations with fruit vendors, community groups, and processing stakeholders. Upon securing these strategic associations, the project will move into its pilot execution phase to validate operations, product viability, and social impact outcomes.
